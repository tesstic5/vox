The audit's D7: *"the interval is one number for the whole practice (`recallDays`, default 180) and there is no 'send the recall' action — the list is a number on a screen."* A5: *"a service should be able to carry its own interval, and the list should be able to act on it."*

Both are closed. The window is now the client's own — measured from the last visit that actually *happened*, against the interval of the service they were last seen for — and the list sends: from the desk, or by the daily loop, with consent, the ceiling, the cooldown and quiet hours all still having their say.

## The contract, exactly

- **The window is data, not a constant.** A service with its own `recallDays` wins; a service with none uses the practice's (`profile.recallDays`, clamped **1–3650** by PATCH; a service may be set to 0 meaning inherit and never negative). The rule's `afterDays` (180) is the fallback when neither is set — and every row says which of the three it used.
- **Measured from a visit that happened.** `lastSeenFor` takes the latest **completed** appointment (falling back to the client's counter only when it is in the past). A client with an appointment booked for next week is still overdue until they turn up — and they are then *held* rather than recalled, with the date named: *"already booked 2026-10-20"*.
- **The list acts.** `send` takes client ids the screen already has (a stale id is a **409**, *"1 of those clients are no longer due a recall — reload the list"*, rather than a quiet partial send); `send-all` sends to everybody due under a ceiling. The desk may overrule a **cooldown** (*"she asked us to remind her"*) and the run says so: `overrodeCooldown: 2`. It may **not** overrule *already booked* or *already on the queue* — pressing send twice is a duplicate, not a decision.
- **Consent is opt-out, and a refusal is a record.** Nothing recorded means nothing refused; an explicit no produces a **blocked** message row in the client's own thread (*"Probe Recall 203349 E has opted out of sms"*) and a line in the run, not a silence. A rule that can only email does not text somebody with no email: *"nothing to send to on the channels this rule uses (email)"*.
- **The message carries the booking page, not a manage link** — a recall's whole point is a *new* appointment — and when online booking is off the link line is dropped rather than sending a client to a door that refuses them.
- **Quiet hours hold it.** A recall queued at 4pm for a practice whose window opens at 5 waits for 5 (`sendAt` moves, the row stays `pending`) — desk-sent or loop-sent alike.
- **The reply comes back to the desk.** A recalled client who answers lands in the desk's own queue with their name and number against the message.

## Defects found while verifying, and what they cost

Seven real defects came out of driving this. Six were in code older than this bundle, and one was a probe consequence of the suite itself.

1. **`?includeInactive=true` returned a 400 and took the Practice page down with it.** `applyListQuery` rejects a parameter it does not know, and `includeInactive` — which the route reads three lines *above* the parser — was not declared in the policy's `passthrough`. Both `/providers` and `/services` answered `400 Unknown filter field`… The Practice page loads its roster, services and sites in one `Promise.all`, and `request()` throws on any non-2xx, so **the whole page failed to load**: no providers, no services, no locations, no diary — and the per-service recall field this bundle adds lives on that page. An earlier pass had met the same trap and written a note in `reports_check` instead of a fix — *"`includeInactive` belongs here only if the route knows it. It does not: the default list is the live one"* was a suite that had learned to walk around the broken door. Now declared on both routes, and a check over every list URL the dashboard calls — **30 endpoints, all 200**.
2. **The Recalls tab's own summary disagreed with the sender.** The tab said *"Would be blocked — no consent — the rules engine refuses these"* about clients who had simply never been asked, and **hid the Recall button** from them — while the sender's own rule is opt-out and would have texted them happily. A practice that imported its clients without consent rows saw a list of people it could not recall, and the button to prove otherwise was missing. The row now carries the sender's own verdict (`reachable`, `blockedReason`) and the cards read *"Consent on file"* and *"Would be refused"*; the CSV gained `Sendable` and `BlockedBecause`, and the report tool's sentence stopped calling silence a refusal.
3. **The list dropped the fields that explain it.** `GET /api/v1/recalls` carried `sendable`/`held`/`blocked` while the reports carried `reachable`/`holdReason`/`blockedReason` — two vocabularies for one verdict, and the second one missing from the list. The list now carries both.
4. **`GET /api/v1/bookings?limit=200` returns the *oldest* 200 appointments.** A gate on a workspace with a year of diary in it therefore never saw today's fixtures — which is why an interrupted run's probe appointments survived sweep after sweep and kept a probe service un-deletable (`409 … still on 1 upcoming jobs`), which in turn tripped `practice_check`'s *"every service on the list is offered by a practitioner"*. The sweep now finds a probe's appointments **through the client** they belong to, and the recall gate deletes probe **services** by name — the probe services had been left on the practice's list.
5. **`practice_check` had a typo that only fired when it mattered.** `all(not (p.get("serviceIds") …) for p in active)` — `active` was never a name in that file. It sat there unseen because the line is only reached when a service is *un*rostered, which is exactly what defect 4 produced. Fixed; the gate is green.
6. **A field on the messaging screen changed nothing.** *"Last-resort window (days)"* is saved as `rules.recall.afterDays`, clamped 7–3650, and reported back by the API as `ruleFallbackDays` — and the resolver it was supposed to feed **hardcoded 180** instead (*"below a service's own and the practice profile's"* was true of the comment, not of the code). It is now passed in from the callers that already hold the rules (`recalls.ts`, `reports.ts`) — the resolver cannot import them, because `messages.ts` imports the practice module and that would be a cycle — and the field says on the screen where it sits in the chain. The gate proves the number reaches the loop as typed.
7. **Probe appointments were cancelled rather than removed, and the demo's attendance paid for it.** `reports_check` books a visit on the most recent closed day to prove the report counts history, and its tidy *cancelled* it — the product's own path, but a cancelled probe is a row nobody attended that stays in the diary for ever. Eleven runs of it put eleven on **2026-10-14** and dropped that day's attendance to **16 of 27 (59%)**, which is what `demo_check`'s *"and most of it turned up"* caught. Probe appointments are now **deleted** at tidy (money is still voided and clients still archived — those are records; a probe appointment is not), and the debris was cleared: **2026-10-14 reads 16 of 16 attended** again, plus 33 more probe rows swept off the diary.

Three gates also assumed a carrier another gate had left behind. A receipt, a reminder and a booking confirmation are only *delivered* through a carrier, the demo workspace ships with none, and `money_loop_check` / `messaging_check` / `booking_check` each failed on the state of the workspace rather than on the code until they learned to borrow the Twilio stub for the run and hand it back — with the hand-back itself asserted.

## What the gate proves, end to end

1. **The window.** A 30-day service puts a client on the list 90 days after a visit; the same 90 days does *not* recall a service that inherits the practice's 180; a 200-day-old visit does; an appointment still to come does not reset the window but holds the recall; the report honours a forced `?recallDays=30` for everybody and reports the practice's own number without it; the services strip names each interval in use.
2. **Recalls that act.** The loop ships off and says so; the console switches it on with a ceiling and a cooldown; the desk sends one and it is **queued**, with the interval it was judged by, the workspace's own wording and the booking link; a batch cannot exceed its ceiling; pressing send twice skips with *"a recall is already on the queue"*; the loop skips the same person; the recall goes out **through the carrier** with an id; the loop then respects the cooldown the desk may overrule, and the run says how many it overruled.
3. **Who is left alone, and who is refused.** Somebody with an appointment; somebody who said no (and the blocked row in their thread); a rule that cannot reach them on its channels; somebody who is not overdue (409).
4. **Quiet hours** hold the recall and move its `sendAt` to the window opening.
5. **The reply** — an inbound text from a recalled client lands in the desk's queue.
6. **Silly numbers** — 99,999 days and 0 are refused by the profile, a negative or non-numeric service interval is refused by the service route, and the practice's own window survives all of it.
7. **The loop twice** — the job queues somebody the desk has not touched, and its second run queues nothing twice.

## What it deliberately does not do

- **No full `send-all` against the demo workspace.** The gate raises the ceiling for its own run and drives the *single-send* path and the *batch ceiling* instead — a real send-all here would text forty demo clients to prove a loop that the job section already proves.
- **No AI-written recall text.** The message is the workspace's own template with the workspace's own vocabulary; the audit's A5 asked for an action, not a new copywriter.
- **No channel beyond sms and email**, and no sending outside the workspace's channel switches.
